# Unemployment in India: Covid-19 impact analysis

## Key findings

- **National shock:** the average state unemployment rate was 9.7% before Covid (May 2019 - Mar 2020) and 22.7% during the Apr-May 2020 lockdown (peak 23.2% in May 2020), about 2.3x higher.
- **Rebound:** by Oct 2020 the rate was 8.0%, still -1.7 pp versus the pre-Covid average.
- **Hardest-hit states (rise in pp, lockdown vs pre-Covid):** Puducherry (+65.8), Jharkhand (+42.9), Tamil Nadu (+38.1), Bihar (+34.2), Karnataka (+21.6).
- **Smallest change (negative = fell):** Uttarakhand (-2.9), Himachal Pradesh (-2.9), Sikkim (-10.2).
- **Participation:** the average labour participation rate fell from 44.2% pre-Covid to 37.5% in the lockdown, consistent with many people leaving the labour force, not only losing jobs.

## Seasonal patterns (read with caution)

- The data holds only 3 pre-Covid months, one cycle, so true seasonality (recurring year-on-year patterns) **cannot be confirmed**.
- Too few pre-Covid months to describe a trend.
- Multi-year data (for example the same months in 2018 and 2021) is needed to separate seasonal effects such as harvest and festival hiring from the Covid shock.

## Policy implications

- **Targeted relief:** states with the largest jumps should get priority for income support and wage-subsidy schemes in future shocks.
- **Both rural and urban need cover:** unemployment rose sharply in both; see the rural vs urban finding above for which was hit harder. Urban employment support for informal and migrant workers and scalable rural public works both deserve consideration.
- **Watch participation, not only unemployment:** a falling participation rate hides discouraged workers, so policy should track both measures.
- **Early-warning data:** monthly state-level data like this lets authorities spot stress within weeks instead of waiting for annual surveys.

## Data notes and limitations

- National figures are **simple averages of state rates**, not labour-force-weighted.
- The two files overlap in Jan-Jun 2020 but have different structure (file 1 splits Rural/Urban; file 2 is overall by state), so their numbers are not merged or directly compared.
- Excluded from the state ranking for lacking pre-Covid or lockdown data: none. Blank rows in file 1 were removed.
- A few state-months are extreme (for example Puducherry's lockdown figures); treat single-state spikes cautiously, as small samples can exaggerate swings.
- Correlation here does not prove causation; the lockdown overlaps with other Covid effects (migration, supply disruption).
